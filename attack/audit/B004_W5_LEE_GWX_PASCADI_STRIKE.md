## 2. Why the time weights do not select a Goldbach target

[P] The kernel of Lee’s quadratic form depends on $m/n$. On $m+n=N$ its argument $x\log(m/n)$ varies with $m$. Conversely a dilation of a pair preserves its ratio and changes its sum. The diagonal frequency alone already identifies all $(n,n)$ with one another. The narrow spatial windows in Lee select a fixed *difference* locally because $x\log(1+j/n)=j/(n/x)+O_j(x^{-1})$. On a fixed window wide enough to contain order $N$ potential Goldbach summands, the required differences $N-2n$ take order $N$ distinct values. Substitution of a varying $h$ would require control of both the constants and spatial resolution, and knowledge of an asymptotic in a long interval would still not specify the prime pair at a given center. [Lee, separation lemma](/tmp/arxiv_nov2026/2610.32672v2/prime_pairs_fixed_even_difference.tex:475)

[P] Reflecting one polynomial explicitly changes the Mellin frequencies. For $u=m/N$ in a fixed compact subinterval of $(0,1)$ the reflected factor has phase $(1-u)^{-it}$. With Mellin convention $u^{iv}$ the phase derivative vanishes at $v=-tu/(1-u)$, whereas $u^{-it}$ has Mellin frequency $v=t$. At $|t|\asymp N$ the correction needed to express the reflected phase about the original frequency has order $N$. The independent additive shifts available in Lee’s integral are only $O(U^\nu)$ at $U\asymp N$, $\nu\le1/4$. An additive Fourier detector $\int_0^1(\sum_{m\le N}\Lambda(m)e(m\alpha))^2e(-N\alpha)d\alpha$ does select the sum, but its signed square is a different input. [I] These calculations rule out the indicated direct substitutions. They do not establish a general impossibility result for zeta statistics.

## 4. A uniform RH bound for the actual four-shift time integral

[P] Here is a bound for the *two-term remainder as Lee defines it*, with independent real and imaginary shifts and a sharp upper endpoint. $$
 \int_0^U \frac{\zeta(1/2+\alpha+it)\zeta(1/2+\beta-it)}

 -\text{Lee's two main terms}
 \ll_\varepsilon U^{1-2d+\varepsilon}
$$
uniformly in the stated box for fixed $\nu>0$. The error here can absorb the entire reflected model. Resolving its coefficient at a strictly smaller scale is a different estimate.

[P] Write the first ratio as a function of a common extra shift $v$. Its Mellin transform has a single simple pole of residue one at zero and rapid vertical decay on fixed strips away from that pole.

[P] The interval $0\le t\le3U^\nu$ costs $O(U^{\nu+\varepsilon})$ for the actual integral by fixed-distance RH bounds for zeta and its reciprocal. All original numerator real parts are bounded away from the pole at one. On $3U^\nu\le t\le U$, insert Mellin inversion on $\Re v=1$ for the polynomial. Truncate at $|\Im v|\le U^\nu/2$ and move to $\Re v=-d+\xi$, where $0<\xi<3/64$ is fixed sufficiently small in terms of $\varepsilon$. The four zeta arguments stay to the right of $1/2+\xi$. ## 5. What ERH reaches for the Goldbach test

[P] For $q>1$ primitive characters are nonprincipal. Apply the same product cutoff to the parity-signed product of $L'/L$ on $\Re s=\Re t=1/2+\eta$. The common Mellin shift to $-\eta+\xi$ crosses the cutoff residue only. ERH gives fixed-distance log-derivative bounds with polynomial or logarithmic height dependence. The family remainder costs $O(q^{1+\varepsilon}Y^{-\eta+\xi}(1+|x|+|y|)^C)$. The retained coefficients are $\Lambda(m)\Lambda(n)$ on units multiplied after character summation by
$$
 b_q(m+n)=\sum_{d_0\mid(q,m+n)}\mu(q/d_0)\varphi(d_0).
$$
On a dyadic $M,K$ box, each contributing $d_0\ll\max(M,K)$ and there are $O(MK/d_0)$ admissible pairs, by fixing the smaller variable. Thus the total polynomial costs $O(q^\varepsilon Y^{1/2-\eta+\varepsilon})$. The model contributes $O(q^{1-2\eta+\varepsilon}(1+|x|+|y|)^C)$ by its two gamma factors and $|M_0-M_1|\le2$. The exact kernel is integrable against these height powers. Optimizing at $Y=q^2$ gives $\iint|K E_q|\ll q^{1-2\eta+\varepsilon}$, reaching the detector boundary. This bound applies to all required composite conductors. [P] One can also see the distinction at the level of undifferentiated Dirichlet ratios. On positive-shift numerator and denominator lines let $d$ be their least real displacement.
